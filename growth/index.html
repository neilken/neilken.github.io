Mesa Vista Growth Lab

Change the number of incoming households to investigate how a community's needs may change.

Fictional classroom model: Mesa Vista, all data, and all calculated effects are invented. They do not describe or predict Gallup. This is a simplified model, not an emergency or planning tool.

More households → housing needs, road trips, water use, and school enrollment → compare demand with capacity

1. Change the scenario

At 120 incoming households, this model matches the worksheet's fictional additions. The model assumes additions change proportionally with household growth and rounds vehicles, students, and gallons to whole numbers. Actual communities may not change this way.

3. Explore the model

Start at 120 households. Compare demand with capacity, then try 60 households. Return to 120 before answering questions based on the original scenario. Some questions give separate hypothetical changes; use each question's numbers.

Definitions, worked examples, and original scenario data

Demand is the amount needed; capacity is the amount a system can provide. Compare the same units and time period. If demand exceeds capacity, shortage = demand − capacity. If capacity exceeds demand, unused capacity = capacity − demand.

Example 1: 420 students + 70 more = 490 students. With 480 spaces, the shortage is 490 − 480 = 10 spaces.

Example 2: A different school has 500 spaces and 440 students. It expects 80 more students and adds 30 spaces. New demand = 520; new capacity = 530; 10 spaces remain.

Percent change: change ÷ original amount × 100. An increase of 30 from 120 is 30 ÷ 120 × 100 = 25%.

Original data: Road: 450 current vehicles + 200, capacity 600 per peak hour. Water: 800,000 + 300,000 gallons per day, capacity 1,000,000 per day. Housing: 300 currently occupied, 120 incoming households; 380 suitable homes total. School: 420 students + 70, capacity 480. Each incoming household needs one home.

Fictional project choices: Option A costs 90 credits and adds 150,000 gallons/day water capacity; B costs 70 credits and adds 30 school spaces; C costs 60 credits and adds 25 homes. Budget is 100 credits. These are separate alternatives.
