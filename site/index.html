Scenario and planning rules

The community plans a new neighborhood. The budget is 100 credits. The new site must have low simulated flood risk, room for at least 100 homes, and enough added water capacity to eliminate a projected 100,000-gallon-per-day shortage.

Without an improvement, projected road demand is 650 vehicles per peak hour, and road capacity is 600 vehicles per peak hour. Added road capacity affects capacity; it does not reduce traffic demand.

School capacity and other effects have not been supplied; planners would need more evidence before a real decision.

Key terms and help

Population growth: an increase in the number of people. Urbanization: growth in the number or share of people living in towns and cities. Growth can increase demand for housing, transportation, water, and services, though the effects depend on local conditions. Land use: how land is used. Infrastructure: physical systems that support a community. Demand: amount needed. Capacity: amount a system can provide. Constraint: a limit a solution must meet. Criterion: a desired result. Trade-off: a benefit paired with a cost or drawback. Interdependence: a change in one system can affect another, such as new housing increasing travel or water demand. Risk: the possibility of an unwanted outcome. Planners consider environmental effects on land, water, and ecosystems; social effects on people and access; and economic effects such as costs and budgets.

Road comparison: new capacity = 600 + added road capacity. Compare the result with 650. Water: compare added water capacity with the original 100,000-gallon-per-day shortage.
